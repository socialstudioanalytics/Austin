The dynamics that matter right now

  • Growth cooled from frantic to healthy. The 2020–22 boom is over; net migration is near equilibrium (~0.7%/yr) with inflows now dominated by Texans (Houston, DFW, San Antonio) rather than coastal transplants. Suburbs (Leander, Georgetown, Kyle, Hutto) are growing far faster than the core.
  • It's a renter's market. Rents fell 6–13% over the past year; ~10% vacancy means concessions (6–12 weeks free) are common. Home prices are flat (city median $595K, metro $440K). Good timing for settling in and for negotiating commercial space.
  • The economy rotated, not stalled. Record $7.19B VC in 2025 (AI, robotics, defense) and a record Q1 2026, while Information-sector employment shrank 2.6% and hospitality shed jobs. Semiconductors (Samsung Taylor fab, 2027 mass production) are the next wave.
  • Office space is a buyer's market; retail is not. Downtown office vacancy 20.6% = deep deals for office users. Retail vacancy is a tight 3.6% — physical-storefront concepts face space scarcity.
  • No state income tax, franchise tax exempt under $2.65M revenue, and no general city business license — among the lowest-friction places in the US to start a company.

Transit reality — what exists, what's coming, and when
  • Austin Light Rail Phase 1 (9.8 mi, 15 stations): 38th St → UT/the Drag → downtown → over Lady Bird Lake, splitting to SoCo/Oltorf and East Riverside/Yellow Jacket. Federally cleared Jan 2026; construction starts 2027; opening ~2033 — a long-term value signal, not a near-term amenity. $7.1B budget; litigation over bond authority still unresolved.
  • Red Line commuter rail (exists today): Leander → Lakeline → Domain area (McKalla/Q2 Stadium, new Broadmoor station opens 2027) → Crestview → Highland → MLK → Plaza Saltillo → Downtown.
  • MetroRapid buses: 801 (N Lamar–S Congress) and 803 (Burnet–S Lamar) spines, plus new 2025 electric lines: Pleasant Valley (Mueller↔SE Austin) and Expo Center↔downtown. No rail to the airport — bus Route 20 or ~15–25 min drive.
  • The I-35 decade: the $4.5B Capital Express rebuild runs through ~2033 straight through downtown — plan around construction disruption on I-35 and prefer Mopac/Lamar corridors for north–south trips.
  • Peak-hour commutes to downtown (est.): East Riverside 10–20 min · Mueller 15–25 · Westlake 15–30 · Circle C 25–45 · Pflugerville 35–60 · Round Rock 40–65 · Cedar Park 40–70. Peak windows ~6:30–9:00 and 16:00–18:30.
  • From your home (Round Rock area): downtown ~20 mi — 30–35 min off-peak, 45–70 peak via 183A/MoPac tolls. The smart alternative: Lakeline Red Line station is 3.5 mi from your door (park & ride, ~50 min to downtown, ~$3.50) — it stops at Q2/McKalla and the Domain-adjacent Broadmoor station (2027) on the way.

The 2026 labor market in one story
  • The "Big Stay": nobody's quitting — quits are 14% below pre-pandemic and turnover is the lowest in ADP's nine-year series. The pay premium for switching jobs has collapsed from 7.7 points (2022) to ~2. It's a low-hire, low-fire, frozen market: hard to break in, safe once you're in.
  • The AI whiplash: 20+ companies cited AI in 2026 layoffs (~140K tech cuts YTD — Oracle 21K, Amazon 16K, Dell 11K, Meta 8K), yet AI-exposed job postings are now growing faster than the overall market, and software-dev postings have rebounded +15% since early 2025 — but 71% of those are senior roles. AI is squeezing the entry rung, not the profession.
  • The barbell is a labor story too: what grows in Austin is physical (construction +6.1% — the metro's fastest sector — semiconductors, data centers, healthcare); what shrinks is digital-adjacent white collar (information −3.2%, hospitality −2.1%, manufacturing −1.3%).
  • Wages: Austin average hourly $38.14, +6.7% YoY — triple the Texas rate — but posted wages nationally are rising slower than inflation for the first time in years. Hot-skill premiums are widening while everything else stagnates.

Where the shortages are — employers can't fill these
  • Nursing (the biggest): Texas is short ~44,700 RNs today, projected 56,400 by 2036; US nursing schools turned away 93,176 qualified applicants in 2025 for lack of faculty/clinical slots. Also severe: medical assistants, surgical techs, radiology techs, respiratory therapists (Workforce Solutions "severe shortage" list).
  • Skilled trades: Austin needs 10,000+ new skilled workers per year through 2040; $25B of construction is underway (airport $5B, Convention Center $1.6B, light rail $7.1B) plus the data-center wave. Entry electricians start ~$45K and reach six figures within ~4 years; hires take 30–90 days.
  • Semiconductor technicians: Samsung Taylor needs 800–1,200 hires in 2026 (2,000–3,000 at full ramp, contractors 3–4×); technical roles average 78 days to fill vs 52 for general tech; senior lithography roles carry $50–75K signing bonuses. ACC's 4-week cert and TSTC's $47M expansion are the pipelines.
  • Teachers — statewide, with an asterisk: 52% of first-year Texas teachers are uncertified and shortages are real across the state — but Austin ISD itself is cutting ($181M deficit, layoffs 2026). The hiring is in suburban districts (Round Rock, Leander, Hays).
  • Accountants: the national CPA pipeline shortage persists into 2026 — time-to-fill for accounting roles keeps stretching.
  • Physicians & childcare: covered in Living Here — both structurally short.
Where job seekers outnumber jobs
  • Entry-level tech is the hardest market in a generation: Big Tech entry hiring is down ~65% vs 2019 (startups −76%); recent CS grads run ~6.1% unemployment — above the all-grad average of 5.7% — and 41.5% of recent grads are underemployed. US tech postings sit 27–36% below pre-pandemic.
  • Austin tech specifically: big-tech employment here fell 1.6% and startup employment 4.9% (2024) as AI hiring concentrated in SF; the Information sector is down 3.2% YoY. Oracle, Dell, Meta and Amazon cuts all touch Austin.
  • Marketing, design, PM: down 36%, 48%, and 39% respectively at large tech firms vs 2019 — the roles AI tools hit first. (Flip side for you: it's a buyer's market for exactly the talent Social Studio would hire.)
  • Hospitality: −3,200 Austin jobs YoY amid the Convention Center closure and SXSW contraction — recovery expected closer to 2029.
  • Office/admin support: nationally flat-to-shrinking as AI absorbs coordination work; Austin postings skew toward roles requiring only a HS diploma at mid-wage — competition there is intense.

The employer lens — for when Social Studio hires

  • Who you compete with for talent: Austin's top posters right now are St. David's HealthCare, Amazon, UT Austin, Tesla, and Ascension — healthcare and institutions, not tech startups. For marketing/creative/analyst roles, the layoff wave means exceptional candidates at reasonable prices — the best hiring window in years for your kind of company.
  • Benchmarks: US average time-to-hire is 44 days (tech 35–50); interview processes have crept to ~4 rounds — a small firm that decides in a week wins talent on speed alone. Signing bonuses appear in only ~2.6% of Austin postings.
  • Market rates (2026 cross-checks): software engineer ~$144K base / $160K total · social media manager ~$69K average (Indeed, range to $101K — budget $70–85K for strong bilingual talent, est.) · full salary table in the Digital Economy tab.
  • Contract-first is normal now: even Samsung runs 3–4 contractors per direct hire. Starting analysts as contractors before FTE conversion is standard practice and fits a services firm's cash flow.
  • Your bilingual edge in this market: Austin's 60.7%-degreed, 21.6%-foreign-born workforce plus UT's Arabic program and Middle Eastern studies center is a genuine local talent pool for Arabic/Hebrew analyst roles — one most US cities can't match.

The barbell — read this before picking a lane
  • One end is contracting: Oracle's March 2026 layoffs (20–30K globally, incl. Texas), Dell's multi-year cuts, gaming-studio layoffs at every Austin AAA anchor, BigCommerce restructuring. Legacy enterprise tech is shrinking here.

First 90 days as a Texas resident

Schools — where the good districts are

Matters twice: personally if family plans are in your future, commercially because school quality drives where higher-income families (your customers and employees) cluster. Austin ISD is mid-crisis: enrollment down 14K over a decade, 11 school closures voted for 2026–27, and Texas's new ~$10K voucher program is accelerating the shift.

Healthcare access — the real map
  • Expect a wait for primary care: Texas ranks 7th-worst for physician access (205 per 100K vs 245 national); new-patient waits average ~a month. Get established with a PCP early, not when you're sick; urgent care fills the gap (dense along I-35/MoPac corridors).
  • Three systems compete hard — Ascension Seton (largest; Dell Seton is the Level-I trauma center; $800M Williamson Co. buildout), St. David's/HCA (~$1B expansion: new Leander + Kyle hospitals), Baylor Scott & White (growing fast). Competition = leverage; cash-pay pricing varies wildly between them.
  • The next decade's anchor: UT's new Dell Medical Center — a 300–500-bed hospital with MD Anderson cancer care in North Austin — breaks ground late 2026, opens ~2030.
  • The gap: Southeast Austin/Del Valle has no full-service hospital — relevant if you settle or locate there. Mental-health capacity is expanding (Ascension-Acadia JV, new St. David's behavioral facility) but waitlists persist.
  • Dell Children's (Mueller) is the pediatric anchor, with a second campus coming in North Austin.
Community & cultural infrastructure
  • North Lamar International District (N Lamar/Rundberg) is the city-recognized global corridor: Chinatown Center with MT Supermarket (largest Asian grocery in Central Texas), Vietnamese, Chinese, Indian, Middle Eastern, and Latino businesses side by side.
  • International groceries: H Mart (Lakeline) and 99 Ranch in the northwest; Patel Brothers, India Bazaar and MGM along the N Lamar/Parmer belt; Fiesta Mart citywide — north Austin is the international-food capital of the metro.
  • Faith infrastructure: ~13 mosques across the metro (Islamic Center of Greater Austin, Nueces Mosque near UT, NAMCC North Austin, plus Round Rock, Cedar Park, Pflugerville, Leander and Kyle centers); an established and growing halal restaurant scene; major Hindu temples with full festival calendars; churches of every tradition.
  • Festivals: Round Rock Diwali Fest is the regional festival of lights; UT and community Diwali, Lunar New Year at Chinatown Center, and a deep Cinco de Mayo/Día de los Muertos calendar east side.
  • Business angle: a 21.6% foreign-born city with visible cultural districts is a market where multicultural positioning is mainstream — and the international corridors have notably lower commercial rents than the trendy districts.

Climate reality — what nobody warns you about
Days at or above 100°F per year (Camp Mabry station)
  • Cedar fever (Dec–Feb) is the newcomer shock: Ashe juniper pollen causes fever-like allergy symptoms. Oak follows in spring, ragweed in fall — Austin's allergy calendar is effectively year-round. Antihistamines + HEPA filter early.
  • Summer (Jun–Sep): plan life before 10am and after 8pm; patio businesses feel a real "heat fatigue" slowdown; 2023's 71 triple-digit days was the stress test.
  • Grid & freezes: post-Uri, Texas tripled battery storage — the Jan 2026 freeze passed without incident and ERCOT rates summer-2026 emergency risk under 1%. A hard freeze still hits most winters: insulate pipes, keep a car charger.
  • Hail (Mar–May): the May 2025 storm caused metro-wide roof damage — check comprehensive coverage on your car, and roof clauses in any lease.
  • Water: lakes refilled in 2025; baseline conservation watering rules stay in effect permanently.

The wedge — why this can work
  • The gap is real: no US-marketed vendor combines native dialectal Arabic + native Hebrew + analyst-grade Palestine/Israel context. The incumbents are either MENA-locked (Lucidya, Crowd Analyzer — no US go-to-market), English-first suites with documented Arabic weakness (Brandwatch, Sprinklr, Talkwalker), or government-scale machine-translation platforms (Babel Street, Zignal) that detect but don't understand.
  • Your product is the analyst, not the dashboard. Machine translation reads words; you read subtext, dialect, code-switching, and context from both sides of the conflict. Sell human-grade interpretation on top of defensible data collection — don't compete with $50K/yr SaaS on software features.
  • Language positioning matters in 2026 America: frame the offer as "regional narrative risk & Middle East intelligence" — not "counter-disinformation." The counter-disinfo market was defunded and politicized (see the funding map below); narrative risk for corporates and research institutions is where the budgets moved.
  • Neutrality is your asset — protect it deliberately. Buyers exist across the entire spectrum of this space. A published methodology + ethics/collection policy, evenhanded sample work, and conscious choices about which lanes you enter (see the government-lane note below) are what let a Palestine/Israel-focused firm serve premium clients on all sides.

⚠ Compliance first — read before touching any campaign dollar
  • The foreign-national rule (52 USC 30121): foreign nationals may not contribute, spend, or — the part that catches consultants — "direct, dictate, control, or directly or indirectly participate in the decision-making" of any campaign, PAC, or party. Green-card holders are treated as US persons and are exempt. Where you stand depends entirely on your immigration status.
  • What's clearly permitted: bona fide commercial services sold at documented fair market value — data, monitoring, analysis delivered as a vendor. What's dangerous: sitting in the strategy meeting. Cambridge Analytica's FEC case (MUR 7351) was precisely about foreign staff participating in campaign decisions; a Canadian executive's $975K fine hinged on "directly participating in internal discussions."
  • The clean structure if you pursue this: US entity · all election-related decisions made by US-citizen/LPR personnel · non-US staff confined to technical/linguistic production · documented market-rate pricing · zero foreign funding touching election work · political-law counsel review before the first contract (~$3–7K est. — the best money in this section).
  • Visibility is structural: FEC filings itemize every vendor publicly — any campaign or PAC client makes Social Studio searchable on fec.gov. In your niche, serving one side's committees will be read as alignment by the other. 501(c)(4) issue work and nonpartisan integrity monitoring carry less disclosure exposure than committee work.
  • FARA still applies: any election-adjacent work financed by a foreign government or party during the cycle means DOJ registration and headlines. Screen ruthlessly.

Compliance & risk — read before signing anything
  • OFAC: analyzing and reporting on sanctioned entities (Hamas, PIJ et al.) is generally permitted under the informational-materials exemption; providing services to them or their proxies is prohibited. Screen every client and payer against the SDN list — especially Gulf and West Bank/Gaza-linked counterparties. Sanctions rounds touching this space landed in Jun 2025, Oct 2025 and Jan 2026; the landscape moves.

  • Reputational symmetry: in this niche, every client choice is read as a statement by someone. A public ethics & collection policy, published methodology, and consistent evenhandedness aren't compliance decoration — they're what makes the business durable. The zoning & strategy notes
  • Zoning determines your use. Most retail/restaurant uses need GR or CS zones; CS-1 specifically for bars/liquor. Check any address's zoning before signing an LOI — it's on the city's property profile tool.
  • Corrections are the real delay: each review-correction cycle adds 2–4 weeks and sends you to the back of the queue. A permit expediter ($2–5K est.) usually pays for itself on a build-out.
  • 60–70% of permits are same-day express — light refreshes (paint, fixtures, signage) often avoid the queue entirely. Ask your landlord what previous-use permits exist; a "change of use" triggers full review.
  • The suburbs are structurally faster: Hutto now guarantees 10-business-day commercial reviews; Round Rock and Cedar Park run streamlined tracks. If speed-to-open matters and your market allows it, that's a real siting factor.
  • Home-based needs none of this — protected statewide (HB 2464), no city permission required.
